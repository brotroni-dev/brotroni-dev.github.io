Capture

Expanding mobile scanning beyond one room

Extending capture from one room to a whole connected plan.

Mobile scanning worked well for a single room - but real projects rarely stop there. Multi-room mattered because it removed the one-room limit and made mobile capture useful for complete, connected floor plans: a more continuous on-site workflow for pros, and a step toward completing full floor plans on mobile rather than treating mobile as a single-room capture tool.

Role / Product Designer Scope / Multi-room flow, in-scan interactions, prototyping, on-device validation Platform / Mobile
Overview

When one room wasn't enough

Mobile scan brought floor plan creation into the space itself: pros could capture walls, corners, and openings on site. But real renovation projects often extended beyond a single room.

Problem

The scan stopped at one room

For connected layouts, pros had to move back to desktop and manually assemble rooms into one plan.

Goal

Keep the scan moving across rooms

Enable pros to continue from room to room on site, while building one connected plan without losing momentum or orientation.

The workflow

From mobile capture to desktop completion

Pros could scan a single room on mobile, then continue the full plan later on desktop - one job split across two places, with the connecting work left to do by hand.

Before

Original mobile scan flow

Existing experience before the redesign.

The flow was optimized for capturing one closed room quickly and accurately.

The challenge

The challenge wasn't scanning rooms - it was connecting them

Real renovation projects rarely happen one room at a time. To support full spaces, the product needed a way to connect separate scans within one shared spatial map. But introducing connected scans also meant introducing more guidance, decisions, and recovery moments throughout the flow.

→

Geolocation was meant to bridge the gap - estimating where each room scan was captured, so separate scans could line up and connect into one floor plan.

The friction

Death by dialogs

Helpful alone. Heavy together. What started as guidance became friction, forcing users to stop, read, confirm, and repeat.

Gaps in the map

Geolocation was intended to give each room a shared spatial reference. But in practice, that reference didn't always hold. When it drifted or broke, rooms behaved like separate local scans - making alignment, rotation, and stitching unreliable.

The flow had to work even when the map didn't

A tutorial could explain how to connect rooms, but it couldn't remove the uncertainty created when the shared spatial reference drifted. Instead of depending on perfect alignment, the experience needed to help users adjust, recover, and continue.

The approach

Making multi-room scan feel continuous

That shifted the design problem from teaching the feature to making the flow resilient. I focused the redesign on keeping users inside the scan, making corrections available in place, and separating the decision to continue from the decision to finish.

01

Guidance within the flow

Instead of stopping users with separate instruction screens.

02

Adjust without leaving

Instead of sending users out of the capture flow.

03

Separate continue from finish

Instead of making one "Next" button carry two decisions.

01Guidance and actions stayed inside the scan

The new flow replaced a single "Point" action with a persistent bottom action bar. The guidance line evolved from a simple white dashed edge into a richer turquoise line system, making edges, corners, and room continuity easier to understand during capture.

02Refine in place

Users could place doors and windows, fine-tune their position, and adjust their dimensions on the spot - without interrupting the scan.

03Making the next step explicit

The redesigned flow clarified two different decisions: continue building the plan by scanning another room, or finish the full capture and move to preview.

Learnings

What multi-room scan taught us

The challenge wasn't just scanning more rooms; it was helping users stay oriented, keep momentum, and trust the plan as it grew.

01

Keep users oriented

Users needed to understand where they were as the plan grew across rooms.

02

Guide without stopping

Instructions worked best when they appeared inside the scan, not as extra dialogs.

03

Let users keep moving

Users needed to fix, continue, or finish without getting blocked.
