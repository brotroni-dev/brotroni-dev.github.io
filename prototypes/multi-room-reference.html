Capture

Expanding mobile scanning beyond one room

Extending capture from one room to a whole connected plan.

Role / Product Designer Scope / Multi-room flow, in-scan interactions, prototyping Platform / Mobile

Mobile scanning worked well for a single room — but real projects rarely stop there. Multi-room mattered because it removed the one-room limit and made mobile capture useful for complete, connected floor plans: a more continuous on-site workflow for pros, and a step toward completing full floor plans on mobile.

Why it was hard

The challenge wasn't scanning rooms — it was connecting them. To support full spaces, the product needed to connect separate scans within one shared spatial map — which meant adding more guidance, decisions, and recovery moments throughout the flow.

The friction

Death by dialogs

Guidance that was helpful alone became heavy together — forcing users to stop, read, confirm, and repeat.

The approach

Making multi-room scan feel continuous

The redesign kept users inside the scan, made corrections available in place, and separated the decision to continue from the decision to finish.

01 Guidance within the flow

The old instruction screens explained the process before the user performed it, then disappeared exactly when guidance mattered most. Moving guidance into the capture screen let me support users in context — replacing memorized instructions with immediate feedback, so users stayed oriented and kept momentum.

Learnings

Scanning more rooms wasn't the hard part. The real challenge was making the flow resilient enough to handle uncertainty without forcing users to stop or start over. Guidance, correction, and recovery needed to happen inside the task, so the plan could keep evolving without feeling fragile.
